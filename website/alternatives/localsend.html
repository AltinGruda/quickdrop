We designed QuickDrop around the single most common LocalSend pain: the phone that "never shows up" or a transfer that stalls on a big video. No discovery to fail means the two devices are almost never the reason a move doesn't work.

Choose QuickDrop when

  • You want files off a phone onto one computer.
  • The person holding the phone shouldn't install anything.
  • Discovery has failed you (VPN, firewall, "devices can't find each other").
  • You want proof a big file finished before it's saved.

Choose LocalSend when

  • You need phone to phone, or computer to computer.
  • You want folders, clipboard, or both directions.
  • Everyone involved is comfortable installing the app.

Why a no-app, one-way tool matters

Most "send files to me" tools ask for an app on the phone, an account, or a cloud upload. That's a lot of friction, especially for someone who isn't technical. QuickDrop removes all of it:

  • Nothing on the phone. It's just a browser page. No install, no update, no permission to grant.
  • Local only. Files go straight from phone to computer over your Wi-Fi. No cloud, no third party.
  • Saved whole, only when complete. A file is checked before it appears, so you never get a half-finished download.
  • Resumes, doesn't restart. Lock the phone or drop Wi-Fi mid-send and it picks up where it stopped.

Frequently asked questions

Does the phone need an app installed to use QuickDrop?

No. You scan a QR code with your phone's camera, a plain browser page opens, and you pick your files. Nothing to install, update, or allow.

How is QuickDrop different from LocalSend?

LocalSend runs installed on both devices and handles many directions. QuickDrop does one job: getting files from a phone onto a computer with zero setup on the phone. It does it without a device-discovery step that can fail.

Is QuickDrop free?

Yes, it's free on Windows 10, Windows 11, and macOS. No account needed.

Do files go over the internet?

No. Transfers happen directly between your phone and computer over your local network. Nothing is uploaded to a cloud or server.

Why won't it work on hotel or office Wi-Fi?

Many public and guest networks isolate devices from each other. QuickDrop works best on home Wi-Fi; if a network blocks it, try a personal hotspot.

Can it move files from my computer to my phone?

No. QuickDrop goes one way, phone to computer, by design. For the reverse or phone-to-phone, LocalSend is the better choice.
